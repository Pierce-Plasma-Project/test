# An explicit non-surjective endomorphism of the Weyl algebra A₃

*Draft note, July 23, 2026. Everything here is verifiable with first-year
graduate algebra: the chain rule, the adjugate formula for a matrix
inverse, induction on words, and evaluation of polynomials at two points.
Every polynomial identity used is also machine-verified
(`experiments/jacobian/dixmier.py`, transcript
`results/dixmier_verification.txt`). The construction is the classical
Dixmier⟹Jacobian machinery applied to the counterexample announced by
L. Alpöge on July 20, 2026 (found with Claude Fable 5); given that
counterexample, the failure of Dixmier's conjecture for n ≥ 3 is known
to follow, so the contribution here is only explicitness and
verifiability. Parallel work is likely.*

## 1. Statement

Let A₃ be the Weyl algebra: the ℂ-algebra generated by
x₁,x₂,x₃,∂₁,∂₂,∂₃ subject to

    [∂ᵢ, xⱼ] = δᵢⱼ,   [xᵢ, xⱼ] = 0,   [∂ᵢ, ∂ⱼ] = 0.        (CCR)

(Physics normalization: pᵢ = −iℏ∂ᵢ.) Dixmier's conjecture (1968) asserts
every algebra endomorphism of Aₙ is an automorphism.

**Theorem.** There is an explicit algebra endomorphism φ of A₃ that is
injective and not surjective. Hence Dixmier's conjecture fails for A₃
(and for every Aₙ, n ≥ 3, by letting φ act as the identity on the extra
generators).

## 2. Ingredients

Let F = (F₁, F₂, F₃) : ℂ³ → ℂ³ be Alpöge's map

    F₁ = (1+x₁x₂)³x₃ + x₂²(1+x₁x₂)(4+3x₁x₂)
    F₂ = x₂ + 3x₁(1+x₁x₂)²x₃ + 3x₁x₂²(4+3x₁x₂)
    F₃ = 2x₁ − 3x₁²x₂ − x₁³x₃

Two facts about F, both checkable by direct computation (and verified in
`verify.py`):

- **(F-a)** det JF = −2 identically, where JF = (∂Fᵢ/∂xⱼ).
- **(F-b)** F(0, 0, −1/4) = F(1, −3/2, 13/2) = (−1/4, 0, 0). Note the
  first coordinates of the two arguments differ: 0 ≠ 1.

Because det JF is the unit −2, the inverse matrix

    G := (JF)⁻¹ = adj(JF) / (−2)

has **polynomial** entries (adjugate formula). The nine entries are
printed in `results/dixmier_verification.txt`; machine check (V1)
confirms JF·G = G·JF = I.

## 3. The endomorphism

Define operators (elements of A₃):

    Xᵢ := Fᵢ(x₁,x₂,x₃)            (i = 1,2,3)
    Dᵢ := Σₖ Gₖᵢ(x₁,x₂,x₃) ∂ₖ     (i = 1,2,3)

**Proposition 1.** The six operators satisfy the CCR:
[Dᵢ, Xⱼ] = δᵢⱼ, [Xᵢ, Xⱼ] = 0, [Dᵢ, Dⱼ] = 0.

*Proof.* [Xᵢ, Xⱼ] = 0: polynomials in the xₖ commute. For the others,
use the two elementary commutator identities in A₃ (both immediate from
the CCR by induction on degree): for polynomials a, b in the xₖ,

    [a∂ₖ, b] = a·(∂b/∂xₖ),
    [a∂ₖ, b∂ₗ] = a(∂b/∂xₖ)∂ₗ − b(∂a/∂xₗ)∂ₖ   (second-order terms cancel).

Then
[Dᵢ, Xⱼ] = Σₖ Gₖᵢ (∂Fⱼ/∂xₖ) = (JF · G)ⱼᵢ = δᵢⱼ  by (V1)/(V2).
[Dᵢ, Dⱼ] = Σₗ ( Dᵢ(Gₗⱼ) − Dⱼ(Gₗᵢ) ) ∂ₗ, where Dᵢ acts on a polynomial
as the derivation Σₖ Gₖᵢ ∂/∂xₖ. Each of the nine coefficients
Dᵢ(Gₗⱼ) − Dⱼ(Gₗᵢ) expands to the zero polynomial — machine check (V3),
or by hand with patience. (Conceptual reason: wherever F is locally
invertible — everywhere, by (F-a) — the derivations Dᵢ are the
coordinate vector fields ∂/∂uᵢ of the local inverse chart u = F(x), and
coordinate vector fields commute; a polynomial identity true on an open
set is true identically.) ∎

**Proposition 2.** Setting φ(xᵢ) = Xᵢ, φ(∂ᵢ) = Dᵢ defines an algebra
endomorphism φ : A₃ → A₃.

*Proof.* A₃ is precisely the quotient of the free algebra on six
generators by the two-sided ideal generated by the CCR. (The monomials
x^α∂^β span the quotient by reordering, and are linearly independent
because they act linearly independently on ℂ[x₁,x₂,x₃] — apply them to
monomials and compare leading terms.) A homomorphism out of a quotient
of a free algebra is exactly a choice of images satisfying the
relations, which is Proposition 1. ∎

## 4. Non-surjectivity — the whole point, in one lemma

A₃ acts on the polynomial ring ℂ[x₁,x₂,x₃] (xᵢ by multiplication, ∂ᵢ by
differentiation). Write w·1 for the action of w ∈ A₃ on the constant
polynomial 1. Let ℂ[F] ⊆ ℂ[x₁,x₂,x₃] denote the subring
{ p(F₁,F₂,F₃) : p ∈ ℂ[u₁,u₂,u₃] }.

**Lemma.** For every w ∈ A₃:  φ(w)·1 ∈ ℂ[F].

*Proof.* im φ is spanned by words in the Xᵢ and Dᵢ, so it suffices to
show ℂ[F] is preserved by each generator's action.
Xᵢ: Fᵢ · p(F) = (uᵢp)(F) ∈ ℂ[F].
Dᵢ: by the chain rule and G = (JF)⁻¹,

    Dᵢ(p(F)) = Σₖ Gₖᵢ ∂ₖ( p(F) ) = Σₖ Σⱼ Gₖᵢ (∂p/∂uⱼ)(F) (∂Fⱼ/∂xₖ)
             = Σⱼ (∂p/∂uⱼ)(F) · (JF·G)ⱼᵢ = (∂p/∂uᵢ)(F) ∈ ℂ[F].

Start the induction at 1 = 1(F) ∈ ℂ[F]. ∎  (Machine check (V4) exercises
this identity on sample polynomials.)

**Theorem.** φ is not surjective: x₁ ∉ im φ.

*Proof.* Suppose φ(w) = x₁ for some w ∈ A₃. Acting on 1 ∈ ℂ[x]:
x₁ = φ(w)·1 ∈ ℂ[F] by the Lemma, so x₁ = W(F₁,F₂,F₃) for some
polynomial W. Evaluate at the two points of (F-b):

    0 = W(F(0,0,−1/4)) = W(−1/4, 0, 0) = W(F(1,−3/2,13/2)) = 1.

Contradiction. ∎

## 5. Remarks

- **Injectivity** (the "quantum mechanics nested inside itself" reading):
  A₃ is a simple algebra (standard first-year fact; Dixmier 1968, or
  Coutinho, *A Primer of Algebraic D-Modules*, ch. 2), and φ ≠ 0, so φ
  is injective. Thus φ embeds A₃ properly into itself.
- **Every verification is finite.** A reader needs: the adjugate
  identity (V1), eighteen polynomial identities (V2, V3), the chain
  rule, and the evaluation (F-b). Each is a mechanical expansion — by
  CAS in seconds (`dixmier.py`), or by hand.
- **Provenance and novelty.** That ¬(Jacobian, n) forces ¬(Dixmier, n)
  via exactly this construction is classical (see van den Essen,
  *Polynomial Automorphisms*, ch. on the Dixmier conjecture; Tsuchimoto
  2005 and Belov-Kanel–Kontsevich 2007 proved the deeper converse
  direction). The object F is three days old. The only possible novelty
  here is the explicit, machine-verified instance; the consequences
  literature now forming very likely contains its own version.
